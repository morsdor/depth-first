## Read this before the list

- **Base rates.** Among videos from channels under 5k subscribers in this dataset — channels that were *picked for
  having had a hit* — the **median video gets about 800 views**; 31 % reach 2k, **7.7 % reach 10k, 1.9 % reach 40k**
  (channels under two years old: median 490, 5.8 % reach 10k). The 44 outliers you were shown are the top ~8 %.
- **The evidence is thin and the corrections cut it down.** Two topics are significant *and* replicate across known and
  small channels (`llm_internals`, `ai_industry_news`); one is significant but cannot be replicated (`data_centers`).
  Out of sample, topic and title features predict nothing for a single video. This list is a ranking by multi-channel evidence, not a forecast.
- **Uncrowded ≠ demanded.** Front pages with no giants (KV cache, CPU-vs-GPU, LLM speed) top out at ~10–40k views;
  front pages with demand (undersea cables, transistors, EUV) are walls of 1M+ videos.
- **Ideas that need a measurement are measurable here:** this Mac, `llama.cpp`/MLX, real power draw — ₹0, no
  generated imagery, the repo's own rule.

## Tier A

### Y01 — Where the watts go in an AI data centre
*Title options (lever: "explained" ×1.32, brand/hardware noun ×1.29):* "Inside an AI Data Center: Where Every Watt Goes" ·
"NVIDIA's Giant AI Rack, Explained: From the Grid to the Chip" · "AI Data Center Power, Explained (Computed)".
*Thumbnail:* A | B verdict frame — **grid watts in | heat out**, one number each, rendered from the scene.
*Computed core:* an electrical chain model (utility → transformer → UPS → PDU → rack → GPU → heat → cooling) in
Python, a 3D walk-through in `@remotion/three`; every loss and the PUE asserted by `emit_ts.py`. Needs **sourced**
rack and PUE figures — none are in this repo.
*Evidence:* topic ×2.82 (90 % interval 1.73–7.12; 20 videos, 8 channels, **7 channels with a 3×+ video**, all but one
video in small channels so it **cannot be replicated** in the known channels); 15.8 % of small-channel videos ≥10k
(2.06× base). Precedents: **Deep Xplain 218k @ 2.7k subs** ("Inside a Data Center: How It Actually Works", channel
6 months old), **Base Config 46.8k @ 2.1k**, Great Lakes Data Racks 31k / 13.6k / 9.2k @ 1.7k, Deep Xplain 11.3k
(cooling), Data Center Richness 16.7k @ 4.7k. Big-channel demand: ColdFusion "Why Building AI Data Centres Isn't
Working Anymore" **1.56M**.
*Front page:* "how data-center power works" — median 130k, 2/10 from 500k+ channels, newest 8 days. **MEP Academy**
(111k subs) has 1.44M on "How Data Centers Actually Work" and three 23–107k follow-ups, so the engineering
explainer is *owned*; the AI-news framing is a wall (TED-Ed 534k, BBC 1.79M, CNBC 1.42M).
*The gap, if there is one:* nobody on the first page computes it — MEP Academy animates, it does not model.
*Risks:* evergreen untestable (the topic is ~2 years old); Gartner's "565 TWh in 2026, +26 %" came from a search
summary of a press release — fetch the primary before using it; rack-power figures vary by source.
*Reel cut:* "one chat answer = this many watts" would need Gate 0 and G3 on its own merits.

### Y02 — KV cache, measured
*Titles:* "KV Cache Explained: I Measured the Same LLM With and Without It" · "Why Your LLM Slows Down After 4,000
Tokens (Measured)" · "KV CACHE: The Memory Every LLM Eats, Explained".
*Thumbnail:* **NO KV CACHE (slow) | WITH KV CACHE (fast)** — the frame Tales of Tensors' 23k video carries today.
*Computed core:* run an open model on this Mac with and without its cache; plot tokens/s against context length;
check the bytes-per-token arithmetic (2 × layers × KV heads × head-dim × dtype bytes) against measured memory; 3D
view of the cache growing. All real, all ₹0.
*Evidence:* `llm_internals` ×1.34 (1.14–1.65; 164 videos, 31 channels, 18 with a 3×+ video; replicates 1.73 / 1.14).
Precedents: **Adam Rosler 40.5k @ 2.2k** (KV cache) and 10.1k (memory wall, 33× his own normal), **Tales of Tensors
23.4k / 15.7k / 13.4k @ 2.7k** (KV cache, FlashAttention, PagedAttention), Clyep 13.6k @ 8.2k.
*Front page:* median 9.5k, 8/10 from channels under 50k subs, newest 17 days; best 196k (IBM Technology), 143k
(Efficient NLP). *The honest ceiling is ~10–40k; only 2.5 % of small-channel LLM-internals videos reach 10k.*
*Gap:* none of the first page *measures* it. *Risk:* crowded with tiny channels; "explained" without a measurement is
what the front page already has. *Reel cut:* the with/without race is a strong 12 s loop shape.

### Y03 — Why LLM speed is set by memory, not compute
*Titles:* "Why Your Mac Runs LLMs at This Speed: The Memory Wall, Measured" · "Tokens Per Second Is a Memory Problem
(I Measured It)" · "LLM INFERENCE: Compute vs Memory, Explained".
*Thumbnail:* **COMPUTE | MEMORY** verdict frame with the one measured ratio.
*Computed core:* roofline model for this machine from its published bandwidth; measure decode speed across model
sizes and quantisations; show tokens/s tracking bandwidth ÷ model bytes. Falsifiable: if the prediction misses by
more than ~25 % the video says so.
*Evidence:* same topic row as Y02. Front page for "LLM tokens per second memory bandwidth": median 7.8k, 7/10 small
channels; Adam Rosler's "Memory Wall" 10.1k at 2.2k subs. *Risk:* low ceiling; hardware numbers date quickly (local-AI
evergreen proxy 0.54, **n = 5**). Verify the bandwidth figure against the vendor's spec sheet.

## How to start (a recommendation, not a decision)

**Three pilots with different risk profiles, pre-registered before posting** — a hand-off from the first two, not three independent bets (the way `r010` and `r019` were):

| Pilot | Why this one | Pass line (day 28) | Fail line |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Y01** data-centre chain | best-supported topic; moderate wall | ≥ 10k views (the top ~8 % of a small channel) | < 2k |
| **Y02** KV cache measured | your stated identity; cheapest build; thin front page | ≥ 10k | < 2k |
| **Y04** what an AI answer costs (capstone: reuses Y01's chain and Y02/Y03's measurements) | the strongest demand signal in the data (Computerphile's token-cost video at 12× its normal) plus the data-centre evidence; swap in **Y05** CPU-vs-GPU if you want a cheaper third | ≥ 10k | < 2k |

For a brand-new channel there is no "own median" to beat, so the pass line is absolute — and 10k is the top ~8 % of the
base rate above, not a median outcome. Expect the other 92 % to look like the median (~800). Set the expectation
before the first upload, not after.

**Each video carries three thumbnails** (A/B in YouTube's Test & Compare, which per vendor write-ups picks on watch-time
share — verify) testing **three different promises**, not three palettes: the verdict frame, the rendered subject
alone, and the one-number frame.

**Funnel.** On the small-channel hit videos, views run at a median of ~14× the channel's subscribers, so subscribers
will not be the funnel; whatever the product is must live inside the video and its description.
