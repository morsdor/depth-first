# Gate 0 — `I70`, "the biggest star in the universe is a speck"

New permanent id, appended to `content_backlog.md` §2 (accent `infrastructure #00D6F7`) on
2026-09-11. **First concept in this repo that did not come from the backlog.** The user's
standing correction, same day: the backlog is a starting point, not the gate — it was written
in one AI-generated sitting and its figures are unverified, so reasoning a topic from scratch
is allowed and the id is appended afterwards so the logs still resolve.

Also the first reel that is **3D** — `@remotion/three` (react-three-fiber inside Remotion),
not the 2D SVG/DOM the first eight were built in. See §7.

## 1. The sentence — approved by the human, 2026-09-11, before anything was built

> **"Everyone shows you how big stars get. Nobody shows you the emptiness — the biggest star
> anyone has found would still be a speck in the gap between two ordinary ones."**

Chosen over two alternatives, both recorded because the reasons matter:

- *"Every solar system picture you've ever seen is drawn wrong — at true scale the poster is
  almost entirely blank paper."* Same emptiness idea one rung lower, but Josh Worth's
  "If the Moon Were Only 1 Pixel" owns it, and the payoff is an absence rather than an object.
- *"Light still takes five hours to crawl from the Sun to Neptune — you can watch it and get
  bored."* Best hook verb, but the payoff is a **wait**, and a reel cannot spend its retention
  budget on boredom by design.

**The spine, and why it is not the generic scale video.** Every one of those does *sizes* and
ends on "and this one is even bigger". Sizes saturate — nothing much passes ~2,000 solar radii,
because a star that large is barely gravitationally bound. Distances do not saturate. So the
ladder **breaks** in the middle, and the break is the rung the genre skips. Same footage the
user asked for (planet → Sun → hypergiant → nebula → galaxy); the difference is that it has an
argument in it instead of a slideshow.

## 2. The payoff frame

`payoff_frame.png`, generated by `mock_payoff.py`. Five minutes of PIL, not a render.

The Sun and Proxima Centauri at each end of the rail-safe width, the whole 4.2465 ly drawn to
scale between them, and the largest known star placed at the midpoint — **0.060 px wide**, so
it needs a ×1,000 magnifier before it is a disc at all. In that same magnifier the Sun is still
0.0281 px, which is the second gut-punch and costs nothing.

Every number on the frame is computed by the script and printed to stdout; none is typed into a
label. The script **asserts its own claims** (`BIGGEST_PX < 1.0`, `FITS > 10_000`) so a bad
constant fails loudly rather than shipping.

## 4. The reach test — and it does NOT pass cleanly. Recorded before the build, not after.

`brand_guide_software.md` §13, three conditions, all required:

|:--|:--|
| **Viewer has personally witnessed the evidence** | **Partly.** They have looked up and seen stars. They have *not* witnessed the gap — what they have actually witnessed is the **genre**, having seen a dozen of these videos. That is weaker than r005's seatback map. |

**So: 1 of 3, and the missing one is the one that separated r005 from r006.** The user chose
this knowing it, for the toolchain — the 3D pipeline is the deliverable as much as the reel is.
Recording it here means that if this lands at r006 numbers, that is a **confirmed prediction and

**Who does the viewer send it to?** The honest answer is "the friend who likes space", which is

is an argument-settler for *"space is mostly empty"* — a claim people do state loosely and get
challenged on.

## 5. Licence — asked next to the friend test, per `I22`

**Clean, and deliberately kept that way.** All five inputs are *published measurements* — facts,
citable (`CLAUDE.md`: published figures are facts; route geometry is a database). No catalogue is
queried and nothing is redistributed, so there is no r006-style rebuild waiting.

Two things Stage 3 must not do casually:

- **If Gaia or SIMBAD is queried for real star positions** for the "solar neighbourhood" rung,
  both are free but both require acknowledgement. Cheap; just has to be recorded.
- **No photograph of a nebula or galaxy.** HST/JWST/ESO images are mostly free to use, so this is
  not a licence problem — it is a **credibility** one. The channel's claim is "the real thing,
  actually computed, not drawn", and a stock nebula would be the first non-computed pixel in
  eight reels. Those rungs get generated from real catalogue positions, or they do not appear.
  **₹0 stands: no image model, and now no image download either.**

## 6. What must NOT be claimed

- **Not "Stephenson 2-18 is the largest star".** Hypergiant radii are model-dependent and
  contested — published values for the same star disagree by up to 2×. Say "one of the largest
  we have measured", or fall back to **Betelgeuse (764 R☉, interferometrically constrained)**,
  which still fits the gap **37,793×** and makes the point without the asterisk.
- **Not "space is empty".** It is empty *of stars*. There is gas, dust and plasma throughout.
- **Not "4.25 ly is a typical gap".** Proxima is unusually *close*. Typical local separations are
  larger — which makes the claim stronger, and saying so is free honesty.
- **No claim about the size of the universe** unless a script computed it.

## 7. Open items — before the build, not before the gate

1. **Float32 depth precision.** The ladder crosses ~30 orders of magnitude; a float32 depth
   buffer degrades after about 7. The camera must **not** actually zoom out. Each rung renders
   in its own local unit system and the scene is re-based between them, cross-faded, with one
   counter carrying continuity across the seam. This decides the JSON/TS data shape, so it is
   settled first, not discovered at render time.
2. **Motion is the top risk, and it is the r006 risk wearing a nicer coat.** Rule 4's blind spot
   is stated exactly: *"a slow push changes pixels without anything HAPPENING"*. A gorgeous slow
   cosmic zoom is the platonic case of that failure — it will pass the 0.35 mean-change rule and
   score badly on event density. Design continuous motion in from beat one and
   **measure per beat at `--width 240`** before changing anything.
3. **The hook must be a promise, not a label** (r008, 2026-09-10). "FIFTEEN WEIGHTS. FIFTEEN
   STRINGS." is accurate and dead. And it must carry the sentence's subject noun — *star* and
   *gap* — or the title is weaker than something already approved.
4. **Runtime 30–60 s.** The ladder has enough rungs to overrun; at ≈6.5 s per idea, 60 s buys
   nine beats. Earn each one.
5. **`@remotion/three` install** — peers on `three` + `@react-three/fiber`, publishes at our
   exact 4.0.512. `<ThreeCanvas>` replaces r3f's `<Canvas>` and locks the GL draw to Remotion's
   frame counter, so renders stay deterministic. Existing chrome composites over it as DOM.

## 9. Stage 3 correction — 2026-09-11. §6 predicted this and it happened.

**The gate's own warning fired within twenty minutes of research.** §6 said *"not
'Stephenson 2-18 is the largest star' — published values for the same star disagree by up to
2×"*. They do, and worse:

- **Stephenson 2-18's 2,150 R☉ does not survive.** It has been revised down toward ~1,400 R☉,
  and the figure was inflated by the ejected-material nebula around the star confusing where the
  photosphere actually is.
- **"The Hayashi limit caps stars at 1,500 R☉" is false and was nearly used.** It is a
  temperature line (~2,500 K) on the H–R diagram, not a radius cap in solar radii, and the number
  came from a fan wiki. Killed before it reached a frame. This is rule 7 — a mechanism *sentence*,
  not a figure, and it would have slipped through every automated check in the repo.

**Replacement, and it is strictly better:** **WOH G64 A, 1,540 ± 77 R☉**, in the Large Magellanic
Cloud — Ohnaka et al. 2024, *A&A* **691**, L15 (arXiv:2412.01921). In November 2024 ESO's
VLTI/GRAVITY made it **the first star ever imaged in detail outside our own galaxy**. So the
object is measured, recent, citable, and has a real photograph behind it.

**The headline number moved 13,430 → 18,749**, and the claim got *stronger* while getting more
honest. Backlog row updated.

**And the theory claim is replaced by an observation.** Rather than asserting a physical size cap,
the reel shows the five largest stars ever measured — WOH G64 A 1,540±77, VX Sagittarii 1,556±110,
RSGC1-F01 1,530(+330/−424), RSGC1-F04 1,422(+305/−390), VY Canis Majoris 1,420±120 — found across
two galaxies and **all within about 10% of each other**. Sizes saturate as a *measurement*, which
is what the spine needed and what this repo is supposed to do. Wikipedia's own caveat rides along:
"All the sizes stated in these lists have inaccuracies and may be disputed."
