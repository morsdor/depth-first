## Pass 2 — Sequence design

### Director's statement

The video is **one continuous journey down a wire**, and the sequence is built so the viewer
never loses which way they are travelling. Two axes carry the whole film: **demand flows
left-to-right** (the human and the question are always LEFT, the machine always RIGHT — the
same axis the shipped thumbnail uses, so packaging and video agree), and **power flows
right-to-left** back down the chain. Every rung up the dazzle ladder is a **pull-back**, never
a push-in: pushing in says *look closer*, pulling back says *it is bigger than you thought*,
and this video only ever has the second thing to say.

The arc runs **intimate → industrial → planetary → intimate again**. It opens on the smallest
object in the film (a phone, `study`) and closes on it (`scene_72`, match-cut back), so the
last frame rhymes with the first. Between them the shot sizes climb with the ladder.

**The expensive moves are rationed to the three places that earn them.** Pull-backs: the
rack→town multiply (`24`), one datacenter→a continent of them (`44`), and the final
phone→reactor chain (`73`). Match-cuts: datacenter tower→**power plant** tower (`31`, where
the match *is* the argument — the water is upstream), H100→the blank wafer it was cut from
(`36`), and the reactor→the phone screen (`72`). One crossfade in 77 scenes (`56`, 1979→2019)
— the only place time actually passes.

**The climax is deliberately still.** `scene_63` carries the title payoff and has *no camera
move at all*. After eleven minutes of escalation, stopping is the strongest thing available.

### Axes — written here, obeyed everywhere downstream

- **`query_flow`** — LEFT-to-RIGHT. The human/question side is always LEFT; the machine/infrastructure side is always RIGHT. This matches the shipped thumbnail (phone lower-left, machine right) — packaging and video share one axis.
- **`power_flow`** — RIGHT-to-LEFT. Electricity travels from generation (right) back through the grid to the datacenter and finally the phone (left). Demand goes right; power comes back left. Never cross these.
- **`scale_ladder`** — Every rung UP the dazzle ladder (rack -> plant -> fab -> nation -> reactor) is a PULL-BACK, never a push-in. Pushing in means 'look closer at this'; pulling back means 'it is bigger than you thought'.
- **`training_vs_running`** — training = LEFT bar, running/inference = RIGHT bar (scenes 52-53)
- **`maps`** — north up; terrain in graphite #2A3240, unlit; the active region carries accent #4DA3FF and nothing else does

### Accuracy invariants (carried from the thumbnail gate)

- **`cooling_tower_plume`** — CONDENSING WATER VAPOUR only — bright white/pale blue, soft-edged. Never smoke, soot, exhaust, storm cloud, or lightning. A fission plant burns nothing.
- **`power_flow`** — steady glowing conductors and cable runs. NEVER lightning bolts or electrical arcing. Do not use the word 'arcs' in any prompt — it renders as lightning.
- **`three_mile_island`** — TWO towers. Unit 2 = the 1979 meltdown; Unit 1 = the undamaged twin being restarted. Never depict the melted unit restarting.

## Pass 4 — Build decisions

**42 `plate` · 35 `assembly` · 0 `plate+layers`.**

The single most consequential decision in this pass: **every schematic, chart, counter and map is `assembly` — built in Remotion from code, not generated as an image.** That is 35 of 77 scenes needing no AI generation at all. It is cheaper (₹0 vs ~₹260), sharper (vector, not upscaled raster), animatable per element (build-ons, count-ups, draw-ons — impossible on a flat plate), and it is the only way the numbers can be *correct*, since an image model cannot be trusted to draw a bar chart to scale. Plates are reserved for what only an image model can give us: cinematic industrial scenes.

## Pass 5 — Assets

### The batch is EMPTY. No charged asset generation, no gate to open.

Every scene resolves to either a full AI plate (pass 4) or a code-built Remotion composition. No scene needs a library cutout, so `layers[]` is empty across all 77 scenes.

**This is not an oversight — it follows from the subject.** `assets_library/` holds The Engineering Atlas's world: warm parchment, charcoal outlines, skin tones, villagers, war elephants, banyan trees. Depth First is ink-black schematic infrastructure with **no recurring characters at all**. Nothing in the existing library transfers, and nothing in this video wants a reusable cutout — the repeated objects (towers, racks, the phone) are *plates* whose framing changes every time.

⚠ **Consequence worth knowing:** Depth First starts with an empty asset library and gets no compounding reuse discount for now. EA's per-video cost falls as its library grows; this channel's won't, until enough videos share objects to justify a library of its own.
