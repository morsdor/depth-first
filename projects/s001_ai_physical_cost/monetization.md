## The core advantage: software *is* the premium ad lane

EA's own CPM table (`channel_strategy.md` §1) calls the tech row "a nice premium *slice*, not the core":

| Content type | Tier-1 CPM |
|:--|:--|
| Physical infrastructure | $5–10 |
| Engineering disasters | $6–12 |
| **Ancient CS / info systems** | **$8–20** — tech/SaaS advertisers pay premium |
| Systems & logistics | $8–15 |

For this channel, **that $8–20 row is every video, not a slice.** Software/dev/AI content sits in the
platform's highest-value advertiser pool (cloud, dev tools, AI infra, security, courses, job boards), so
the whole stack shifts up versus EA.

## Revenue stack — ordered by what realistically pays (per EA §6's corrected ordering)

### 1. Sponsorships — PRIMARY lever (bigger here than for EA)
Dev audiences are the most *directly monetizable* audience on YouTube — the viewer of an AI-cost video is
exactly who a database / observability / GPU-cloud company pays to reach.
- Integrated dev/tech sponsor slots commonly run **$20–40+ CPM** (vs ~$5–10 general).
- A video at ~150k views → a **$3,000–6,000** sponsor slot. Two/month at scale → **$6–12k/mo**.
- The pilot topic (cost/infra of AI) is a magnet for AI-infra / GPU / cloud / observability / dev-tool
  sponsors — strong product-market fit from video 1.
- **Timing:** realistically begins ~10k+ subs / consistent 20k+ views. Dev-tool sponsors chase even
  small high-fit audiences, so likely *earlier* than EA's month-18 estimate.

### 2. Owned audience / product — highest margin, deferred until traction
A trusted dev audience buys dev things:
- **Newsletter** — start early; itself sponsorable ($500–5k+/slot) and often out-earns channel ads.
- **Deep-dive courses / paid explainers** (system design, "how X really works") — devs pay for structure.
- **Own tool / SaaS / template / book** — the real ceiling; "channel" becomes "business." Uniquely
  available because the audience *are* the buyers.
- **Rule (EA §6):** don't build speculatively — build once data shows what resonates.

### 3. AdSense — passive background, not the main event
- RPM ≈ 40–55% of CPM → a Tier-1-heavy software channel lands ~**$5–10 RPM** mature (above EA's $4–8,
  because software is the premium row).
- **Tier-1 progression isn't day 1** (EA §5/§4b): a new India-based channel starts India/Tier-2-heavy at
  **$1.5–3 RPM** and climbs as the audience globalizes. Software likely climbs faster (dev content
  self-selects a global/Tier-1 audience) — but it's *earned*, via §4b's framing/seeding rules.
- **Few-videos model → ads accrue slowly by volume, but evergreen tech compounds.** ~20 evergreen videos
  × 20k views/mo = ~400k monetized views/mo × $6 ≈ **~$2,400/mo passive**.

## The risk that's WORSE here than for EA

The 2026 "inauthentic content" demonetization sweep (EA §4a) targets exactly "AI narration over AI images,
batch-produced from a template." An **AI-leveraged channel about AI** is squarely in the blast radius if
the pipeline shows. Non-negotiable defenses (same as EA):
- **Your own voice from day 1** (authenticity signal + differentiation).
- **Visible human judgment in every frame** (accuracy, restraint, craft).
- **Few-but-crafted**, never batch/templated volume.

This isn't only quality — it's what keeps the entire revenue stack monetizable.

## Bottom line

A *better business* than the Engineering Atlas: premium advertiser lane, the most sponsor-hungry audience
on the platform, and a product ceiling that includes shipping software to the exact people who'd buy it.
The "few high-quality evergreen videos" model fits perfectly — every video is a durable, high-value
sponsorship asset that compounds, not a bet on ad volume. Ads alone won't replace a salary; **sponsorships +
owned audience (newsletter → product) carry the real money**, on a 2–4-year build.
